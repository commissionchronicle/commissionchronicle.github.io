The Lead

Put Out of the Table, Capone Answers With Names

Removed from the LCN on his own telling, his ceasefire cancelled the next day, the Capone godfather spent the week doing what he has always done — publishing names.

City Desk • Filed Tuesday night

Los Santos — In reels dated Tuesday, November 17, 2026, the Capone godfather claimed the assassination of a Marcello figure, performed by himself, and, the same day, the assassination of the Marcello consigliere, also performed by himself. Both claims are Capone’s alone, unconfirmed; the Marcello family has published no answer. The development is in the detail: when Capone claimed a triple assassination of Marcello members on November 8, 2026, he named no victims; this week the claim has names and a rank.

In a reel dated Saturday, November 14, 2026, Capone claimed the assassination of a Marzoni figure, performed by himself — the second claim on that figure, after Muller claimed the same assassination in a reel dated November 5, 2026. The Marzoni family has answered neither claim; the target’s own account posted, without captions, on November 15 and 16, 2026. Both claims stand claimed and unconfirmed.

The week began with Capone’s own statement, in a story dated Friday, November 13, 2026, that his family had been removed from the LCN — “We have not been blacklisted but we have been removed.” No LCN-side statement confirms the removal; it stands on Capone’s word.

On Saturday, November 14, 2026, O’Rilley announced in his own story: “Ceasefire Agreement with Capone Family is no more.” On Sunday, November 15, 2026, Capone claimed his family “bested” O’Rilley in a 2v2 CB; O’Rilley’s stories the same day called Capone out and conceded nothing. The win is claimed and contested; no war has been declared by either side.

Second Lead

Five Names, One Signature: The La Concordia Claim

Los Santos — In a post dated Thursday, November 12, 2026, a Neopolo figure announced “the La Concordia Commission — Neopolo • Muller • Vercetti • Lucianno • Corleone. Five families coming together through mutual respect, loyalty and a shared vision for the future.”

The Chronicle notes, without drawing a conclusion: the Marzoni godfather, in a statement dated November 14, 2026, treated the name as a punchline — the community “is not defined by LCN La Concordia or whatever they’re gonna call themselves in a week” — and an account carrying the Luciano name spent Tuesday, November 17, 2026 publicly mocking Neopolo’s authority in another matter (see the Merlino story below). The Chronicle reports the claim; it does not certify the table.

The Merlino File Reopens: A Deadline Passed, a Brother Put Out, a Clearance Disputed

Los Santos — The Neopolo–Merlino deadline — a one-week ultimatum carried in this paper’s last edition, falling due around Saturday, November 14, 2026 — passed with no Neopolo statement lifting it and none enforcing it. For the record, precisely: the clearance printed last week was one officer’s. A Neopolo officer said in a story dated November 9, 2026, after speaking with the Merlino boss, that Merlino had “nothing to do with recent events” and that the wrongdoing “is cleared in my eyes,” offering recompense for his own damages; whether the family’s deadline was lifted was never stated. On Tuesday, November 17, 2026, an account carrying the Luciano name reposted this paper’s own headline and answered it in public: “Neopolo doesn’t clear sht lol.” The paper does not answer story callouts; it corrects its record where the record needs it, and the record is as stated above.

On Saturday, November 14, 2026, the Merlino boss announced in his own story: “Again [a Merlino figure] is no longer associated with the Merlino Crime Family we do not associate with any of the family’s that he does business with or is apart of.”

The expelled Merlino figure answered at length in a story dated Tuesday, November 17, 2026, claiming a Neopolo figure approached the family’s one active member “begging for my removal from their Instagram chat and attempting to orchestrate an assassination against me,” that he was removed and blocked, that “they never managed to take me out,” and that “Merlino has been inactive for approximately five years.” The account is a claim, single-source; Neopolo has published no answer.

The Commission’s View

Every House, Its Own Law

On Thursday, this city changes its address. Half the names in this paper have already packed; the rest are measuring the new ground from photographs. They will arrive in Vice City with everything Los Santos taught them, including the habit that did this city the most damage.

It was never the shooting. It was the law — or rather, the laws. Every house in Los Santos keeps its own book, and every dispute this paper has covered this season was, underneath the grievance, an argument over whose book counts. A ceasefire written under one set of rules dies under another. A clearance spoken by one officer is read as surrender by the next street over. Nobody broke the rules, most weeks. They were simply never playing under the same ones.

A city cannot run on five different laws and call the argument a surprise. Vice City will hand every arriving family the same blank page. What they write on it — one law the whole city can point to, or fifty private ones — will decide whether the new city is a capital or just Los Santos with better weather.

The Chronicle will be watching. It suggests the city do the same.
